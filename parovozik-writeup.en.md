# "The Locomotive": how I set up working with an AI agent around my time and mood

## TL;DR

I built a simple human-plus-AI-agent system: a physical board with a hard cap on tasks, and an agent that suggests **one** concrete action based on how much time and energy I have. No pressure, no endless lists. The system is in daily use, and the main result is that more things get finished and starting is easier, even when I'm low on energy.

## The problem

I had more to do than energy to do it. Tasks dragged on, and papers and small chores piled up, creating a constant background sense of chaos. Ordinary to-do lists didn't help: the longer the list, the easier it is to never open it.

## What I built

The system has three parts:

1. **A physical board** on a shelf in my home office. Two columns: "This Week" (a maximum of **3** cards) and "Done". The hard cap keeps me from taking on more than I can realistically handle.
2. **A paper inbox.** Small items and papers go into one pile, which I periodically hand to the agent to sort, instead of sorting it myself at a moment when I have no energy.
3. **"Locomotive" mode.** I tell the agent how much time I have and what my mood or energy is like. The agent replies with **one** action that fits that state. The rules for the agent: it is a helper and secretary, it doesn't command or pressure me, and it adapts to me.

The split of roles: the human makes the decisions, the agent proposes and narrows the choice.

## What works best

**A physical board in plain sight.** It is always in front of my eyes, and using it requires opening nothing: no app, no tab. Moving a finished card to "Done" is simply satisfying, a small reward that a digital list doesn't give.

## What actually changed

- **More things get finished.** The cap of 3 cards and "one action" replaced a long list that never got closed.
- **It's easier to start, even with low energy.** The agent picks an action that matches my state, so the barrier to starting is lower.

An honest note on numbers: how many cards I close in a week varies, and it isn't always all three. That's fine with me. The cap isn't there to produce a perfect score, but to keep me from overloading myself and to make it clear what matters right now.

## What doesn't work yet

I sometimes forget about the board. The system relies on me going to it, not on it reminding me. That is the flip side of a physical board: it is in plain sight, but if I don't walk into the office, it does nothing to get my attention.

## Takeaways

- A cap on the number of tasks works better than a smart list.
- "One action that fits my resources" removes the problem of choosing.
- A physical element (the board) gives what a purely digital assistant doesn't: it is visible without being asked.
- **Main takeaway:** an agent needs more than capabilities. It needs rules of behavior: don't pressure, don't command, adapt to the person.

## What's next

The next step is to automate sorting of the paper inbox. Right now I hand it over to the agent manually from time to time. I want the sorting to follow a simpler, regular routine, with the same rules: the agent proposes, I decide.
